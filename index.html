The biological identity

An organism’s inherited characteristics are encoded in its genetic information: DNA, organised into genes and packaged into chromosomes.

How DNA shapes inherited traits

Genes carry instructions for proteins and RNAs. Proteins build structures and run chemistry, so the sequence of bases, together with the environment, influences an organism’s characteristics. Offspring inherit copies of DNA from their parents.

Biological continuity

Why mitosis matters

DNA is copied once in S phase. Mitosis then separates the sister chromatids so each daughter cell receives a complete, identical set of chromosomes, and cytokinesis divides the cytoplasm.

That continuity lets one fertilised cell become a body, repair a wound and replace worn-out cells, while every cell keeps the same genetic information.
